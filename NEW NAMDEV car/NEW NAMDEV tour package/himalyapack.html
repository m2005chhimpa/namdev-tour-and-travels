Trekking Expedition · Jammu & Kashmir

Kashmir Great Lakes Trek

The Kashmir Great Lakes Trek is widely regarded as the finest high-altitude walk in the Indian Himalayas, threading through the Sonamarg range and stringing together seven glacial lakes across roughly 72 kilometres of meadows, birch forests, and ridgelines. Each day brings a distinct landscape — dense pine forest gives way to open bugyals (alpine pastures), which in turn open onto turquoise lakes framed by snow. We handle the full logistics chain: certified trek leaders, quality tented camps, all meals on the trail, and the forest permits required for the Sonamarg-to-Naranag crossing, so your only job is to walk and take it in.

8 DaysDuration
7 LakesPlaces covered
ChallengingDifficulty
Jul – SepBest time

Few treks in India pack in as many alpine lakes in one route. Vishansar and Gadsar shift colour through the day, and the Gadsar Pass crossing at over 4,100m is the trek's high point, both literally and in the memories it leaves behind.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

Arrive in Sonamarg, acclimatize, briefing on the route and gear check.

Trek through pine forests to Nichnai, then cross Nichnai Pass to reach Vishansar Lake.

Cross the Gadsar Pass, descend to Gadsar Lake, continue to the Satsar lake cluster.

Trek to Gangbal Lake beneath Mount Harmukh, then descend to Naranag.

Drive from Naranag back to Srinagar, drop-off at hotel or airport.

Gadsar Pass

The trek's highest crossing, opening onto sweeping valley views.

Gangbal Lake

Road Expedition · Himachal Pradesh & Ladakh

Manali–Leh–Ladakh Circuit

Regularly ranked among the world's great road trips, this circuit carries you from the green Kullu valley in Manali, over the Rohtang and Baralacha passes, and into the stark, high-altitude desert of Ladakh — a landscape so different from Himachal that it feels like crossing into another country entirely. Along the way you camp at Sarchu on the border of two states, cross Tanglang La at over 5,300 metres, and finish among the monasteries, markets, and lakes surrounding Leh. Because rapid altitude gain is the single biggest risk on this route, we build in dedicated acclimatization stops at Manali and again on arrival in Leh, rather than rushing the itinerary.

9 DaysDuration
6 RegionsPlaces covered
ModerateDifficulty
Jun – SepBest time

The overland route through Sarchu and the Moore Plains is as much the destination as Leh itself — a stretch of high-altitude desert unlike anywhere else in India. We pace the altitude gain carefully so the journey stays comfortable, not just scenic.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

Arrive in Manali, rest day for acclimatization, short visits to Solang Valley and Old Manali.

Cross Rohtang Pass and Baralacha La, overnight camp at Sarchu on the Himachal-Ladakh border.

Drive across Tanglang La, one of the world's highest motorable passes, arrive in Leh.

Day trip to Pangong Tso, then cross Khardung La into the Nubra Valley for a night among the dunes.

Visit Shanti Stupa, Leh Palace, and Thiksey Monastery around the town.

Transfer to Leh airport for onward flight, or continue independently.

High-Altitude Desert · Himachal Pradesh

Spiti Valley Expedition

Spiti — literally the "middle land" between India and Tibet — is a high-altitude cold desert of ochre cliffs, glacial rivers, and whitewashed monasteries that feels closer to the Tibetan plateau than to the rest of Himachal Pradesh. This circuit takes the classic overland route from Shimla through the apple orchards and terraced fields of Kinnaur, crosses into Spiti proper, and loops back via Manali, giving you both entry and exit through different terrain rather than a there-and-back drive. Along the way you'll pass through villages that rank among the highest motorable settlements on earth, each one still running on centuries-old Buddhist traditions.

8 DaysDuration
5 VillagesPlaces covered
ModerateDifficulty
May – OctBest time

Key Monastery has stood on its hilltop since the 11th century, and Komic and Langza sit among the highest motorable villages on earth. The stark, lunar landscape of Spiti feels closer to Tibet than the rest of Himachal.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

Drive from Shimla along the Sutlej valley into Kinnaur, overnight in Kalpa or Sangla.

Cross into Spiti via Nako, continue to Kaza, the region's main town.

Visit Key Monastery, the fossil village of Langza, and Komic, one of the highest villages with a motorable road.

Drive to Chandratal, the crescent-shaped "Moon Lake," camp overnight.

Cross Kunzum Pass and Rohtang, descend into Manali for departure.

Places You'll Visit

Key Monastery

A centuries-old monastery perched dramatically on a hilltop.

Chandratal Lake

A crescent-shaped high-altitude lake ringed by barren peaks.

Winter Trek · Uttarakhand

Kedarkantha Winter Trek

Kedarkantha is consistently rated one of India's most popular winter treks, and for good reason — it delivers a genuine snow-summit experience without demanding prior high-altitude trekking history. Starting from the timber-frame village of Sankri, the route climbs steadily through snow-laden pine and oak forest, opens into wide meadows, and culminates in a pre-dawn summit push to 3,800 metres for a 360-degree panorama that takes in the Garhwal and Kinnaur ranges together. The gradual altitude gain, well-marked trail, and established campsites make this an ideal first Himalayan trek for anyone reasonably fit and comfortable with cold-weather camping.

6 DaysDuration
3,800mSummit altitude
Easy-ModerateDifficulty
Dec – AprBest time

The summit view takes in the Swargarohini and Bandarpoonch ranges in one sweep, and the campsite at Juda Ka Talab — a frozen lake ringed by pines — is reason enough on its own. It's an ideal first trek for winter beginners.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

Drive from Dehradun to Sankri, the trek's base village, overnight stay.

Trek through pine and oak forest to the frozen lake campsite of Juda Ka Talab.

Continue climbing through open meadows to the base camp below the summit.

Pre-dawn summit push, panoramic views, descend to Hargaon campsite.

Trek down to Sankri, drive back to Dehradun.

UNESCO Trek · Uttarakhand

Valley of Flowers & Hemkund Sahib

The Valley of Flowers is a UNESCO World Heritage site that, for a few short weeks each monsoon, transforms into a carpet of hundreds of alpine flower species set against glaciers and waterfalls — a sight botanists and casual trekkers alike travel from across the world to see. We pair the valley visit with a climb to Hemkund Sahib, a Sikh shrine sitting beside a pristine glacial lake at over 4,300 metres, one of the highest gurudwaras in the world. Both destinations are reached on foot from the same base village, making this a compact but genuinely rewarding trek for anyone drawn to Himalayan flora and high-altitude pilgrimage in equal measure.

6 DaysDuration
2 TreksPlaces covered
ModerateDifficulty

Day-by-Day Itinerary

Trek alongside the Pushpawati river to the base village of Ghangaria.

Full day exploring the flower-carpeted valley, return to Ghangaria by evening.

Steep climb to Hemkund Sahib and its glacial lake at over 4,300m, return to Ghangaria.

Trek down to Govindghat, drive back to Haridwar for departure.

This eastern Himalayan circuit pairs two of India's most distinctive hill regions. Darjeeling brings colonial-era charm, rolling tea estates, and the UNESCO-listed Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, all set against regular views of Kanchenjunga, the world's third-highest peak. Hill Town Getaway · Himachal Pradesh

Dharamshala – McLeodganj – Triund

McLeodganj has been home to the Dalai Lama and the Tibetan government-in-exile since 1960, and that history shapes everything about the town — prayer flags strung between cafés, Tibetan bakeries, and a genuinely international, laid-back atmosphere unlike anywhere else in Himachal. This short getaway blends that cultural side with easy mountain access: mornings spent around the Namgyal Monastery and the town's markets, afternoons at Bhagsu Falls, and a full-day hike up to the Triund ridge for close, unobstructed views of the Dhauladhar range. It's an ideal trip for travelers who want a genuine mountain experience without committing to a multi-day trek.
